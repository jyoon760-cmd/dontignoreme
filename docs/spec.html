Product spec · v0.1 draft · 4 Aug 2026

InvolveMe — a calendar companion that refuses to be ignored.

A desktop app that watches your Google Calendar and, when something is close, takes the whole screen. No banner to swipe away. A small character shows up, visibly excited to be part of your day, and stays until you acknowledge it.

Source: design doc v1 Source: web dashboard mockups (turn 1) Source: shipped Electron build

The bet

Reminder apps fail at the last mile: the notification arrives, you dismiss it reflexively, and you're still late. InvolveMe replaces the dismissible banner with a full-screen takeover and gives the interruption a face — a slightly clingy, extremely earnest creature who takes your calendar personally.

The emotional hook is the product. A generic reminder that seizes your screen is hostile; a small creature who is excited you're about to hang out earns the same interruption. Every decision below should reinforce that the character has feelings about your schedule.

Who it's for

Primary — the chronically late knowledge worker

Works heads-down on one screen, has 3–6 calendar events a day, and misses the first five minutes of most of them. Already gets calendar notifications and has learned to ignore them.

Not for

Teams, shared calendars, or scheduling. InvolveMe never creates, edits, or deletes an event — read-only, single user, one machine. It is a nudge layer, not a calendar.

Three principles

  • Quiet home, loud alert. The everyday screens are calm and cream. All the boldness is spent on the takeover. If the home screen competes, the takeover stops landing.
  • Eager, never guilt-trippy. The companion is excited to see you, not disappointed in you. It may pout; it may never scold.
  • Acknowledgement is the unit of success, not delivery. A notification that fired but wasn't answered is a failed notification.

The loop

  1. ConnectOne-time Google OAuth, read-only calendar scope. Credentials stay on the machine.
  2. Between eventsHome shows next-up with a live countdown, the rest of today as color-blocked cards, and the companion idling at its calmest mood.
  3. EscalateAs the event approaches, the companion walks a colour-and-mood ramp — green → yellow → orange. Ambient, still ignorable.
  4. Take overAt the user's lead time, the app seizes the full screen: giant countdown, event title, companion at full scale, one line of character voice, chime.
  5. AnswerTwo choices only. Acknowledge (dismisses for that event, awards points) or defer (returns in 5 minutes). There is no third exit.

Open decisions

The design doc and the dashboard mockups disagree in six places. Each needs a call before build.

QuestionThe conflictRecommendation
Points & streak Mockups show a points total, a 9-day streak, status tiers (Sleepy Susan → Warming Up → Reliable Regular → Go-Getter), and +10 on acknowledge. The design doc never mentions scoring. Ship it, but keep it in the header only. A score that follows you into the takeover turns an emotional appeal into a chore.
One companion or four Doc specifies a single mascot ("Bud") with customization as a stretch goal. Mockups ship a picker with four named characters at nag levels 1–5. Four. Choosing a companion is the strongest first-run moment we have, and one shape recolored costs nothing to produce.
Button copy Three variants exist: "Got it!" / "Snooze 5 min" (doc), "I'm here" / "I'll get to it" (1d), "I'm on my way" / "I'll get to it" (1c). The doc's own rule says buttons must say exactly what they do. "I'm on my way" / "Remind me in 5". Keeps the character's voice on the primary action, keeps the promise explicit on the secondary.
How much pout "Never guilt-trippy" vs. the mockup's deferred response: an upset face and ...fine. Five minutes. Allow the pout, gate it: neutral on the first defer, pouty only from the second on the same event. Never on a dismissal.
Companion voice lines The "Umma" sample line (a joke about DMing Taylor Swift) is off-archetype for a strict character and won't age. Rewrite. Every companion needs 8–12 lines per mood, scoped to the archetype, no topical references.
Nag level Picker shows a 1–5 dot rating per companion. Nothing defines what it changes. Bind it to one variable only — how many times a deferred alert returns before it stops. Dog = 5, Gentle = 1.

Success signals

Acknowledgement rate

Takeovers answered ÷ takeovers fired. The core metric. Target >90% — a takeover that gets waited out is a bug.

Defer-to-acknowledge ratio

Rising defers means the lead time is wrong for that user, not that the user is failing.

Day-7 retention

The honest test of whether an app that hijacks your screen is welcome. Uninstall is the loudest possible signal.

Watch for

The failure mode is habituation — the takeover becomes the new banner and gets clicked through without reading. If acknowledgement rate stays high while on-time arrival drops, the interruption has stopped working and no amount of extra volume will fix it.

Two visual worlds

The everyday UI stays white, quiet, and almost entirely grey type. The takeover flips the ground to near-black and lets the accents run at full saturation. The contrast between them is the product, so the two should not feel like the same app with a different background — they should feel like a room and a spotlight.

Calm mode

Hand-wobbled linework, white ground, accents used sparingly. Home, settings, empty states.

Alert mode

Yellow radial glow, huge marker type, one creature at full scale. The takeover only.

The escalation ladder

One shape, five moods. Nothing is redrawn between states — only the body colour, the eyes, the mouth, and the arm pose change. This is the single thing InvolveMe should be remembered for.

Rule

Colour never carries urgency alone. The mouth shape, the arm pose, and the countdown number must each independently say how close the event is.

Contrast debt

Only Secondary Grey is a text colour. Against white it runs 11.2:1. Every other colour in the palette fails WCAG AA for body text on white — Primary Orange 3.0:1, Secondary Green 2.8:1, Light Red 2.1:1, Vib Yellow 1.6:1. They are fills, and the mockups use them that way.

On the grey takeover ground the numbers invert: white 11.2:1 and Vib Yellow 7.2:1 both clear AA for body text, Light Red clears it at 5.3:1, and Orange (3.7:1) and Green (4.0:1) are large-text only — fine for the 86px countdown, not for the meta line.

If you need coloured text on white at body size, darken: orange to #B84410 (5.4:1), green to #1A6B48 (6.5:1), light red to #B23636 (6.1:1). Vib Yellow has no usable dark variant that still reads as yellow — use grey instead.

Shape & line

  • 2px Secondary Grey outline on everything. Cards, buttons, the companion, speech bubbles. Slightly hand-traced, never a crisp hairline.
  • The outline inverts on the takeover. Against the grey ground it goes white, so the companion keeps its drawn edge instead of dissolving into the background.
  • Radii: cards 20px, buttons and chips 999px, the companion's own silhouette organic and asymmetric — no sharp corners anywhere.
  • No clocks, no calendar glyphs. Urgency is carried by the companion's posture and the colour ramp. A stopwatch icon would say the same thing twice, worse.
  • Speech bubbles get a rotated-square tail, outlined to match, anchored to the companion's mouth height.

Screens

Home — between events

Two columns on white. Left: the companion on a card stage with a soft Vib Yellow radial glow, a speech bubble at its mouth, and today's timeline as a row of grey-outlined dots below. Right: the Primary Orange next-up card — eyebrow label, event title in marker type, meta line, and the minutes remaining in white at 74px — then rest of today as colour-blocked rows cycling through green, yellow, and light red so the day reads varied at a glance.

Header carries the wordmark, streak, and the status pill. Nothing else. This screen is deliberately quiet.

Takeover — the signature screen

Secondary Grey ground, one large off-centre Vib Yellow radial glow. Companion at 250–330px in Primary Orange with a scale + overshoot entrance (~450ms). Beside it, stacked: Vib Yellow countdown at 86px, event title in white marker type at 38px, speech bubble, then two full-width pills — primary in solid white, secondary as a white outline. After the answer, both buttons collapse into a single result chip (green for acknowledged, light red for deferred).

Two exits only. No close button, no Escape key, no click-outside.

Companion picker

Split view. Left rail: a list of companions, each row a grey-outlined card with a 34px portrait, name, archetype, and five insistence dots. Right stage: the selected companion at 200px on a glow tinted to its own colour, name in marker type, archetype eyebrow, one-line blurb, one sample line in its voice, and a grey pill CTA. Hover previews, click commits.

Footer note, always visible: you can swap companions any time — your points and streak follow you.

Settings & utility screens

Deliberately boring. White ground, plainly listed facts, one full-width grey or orange pill CTA. Lead time as 5 / 10 / 15 / 30 chips. Connection status, sound toggle, companion swap. These screens make the takeover hit harder by contrast — resist decorating them.

Voice

Write as the companion wherever the companion is on screen. Write plainly everywhere else — the character voice must never bleed into settings labels or error states, or the bit stops working.

Yes
Ooh, this one's soon! Can I come? Design review in 42 minutes. I'm already excited. One sentence. Present tense. Excited to see you. Sits inside a bubble.
No
Reminder: Design review starts in 42 minutes. You've missed 3 events this week. System voice and scorekeeping. The companion never sounds like a notification, and never counts your failures back to you.
  • Buttons say exactly what they do. "Remind me in 5" delays by five minutes — not "Later", not "OK", not "Continue".
  • Empty state is the one place the companion gets to be dramatic about having nothing to do. Low stakes, good spot for a joke.
  • Never Alert:, Reminder:, or a colon-prefixed system string anywhere the companion is speaking.

Motion & sound

  • Always breathing. Even at rest the companion has an idle bob (~3.6s). It should never look static — that's what sells "alive and paying attention".
  • Entrances pop, they don't fade. The takeover companion arrives at ~450ms on a cubic-bezier(.34,1.56,.64,1) overshoot. Eager, not polite.
  • Bounce rate encodes urgency: 3.6s idle → 2.1s heads-up → 0.62s excited → 0.5s takeover.
  • Two chimes, not one. Keep the ascending sparkle for the ambient heads-up; add a distinctly more energetic variant for the takeover so the two moments are audibly different.

Accessibility

  • The takeover must be fully answerable without sound — the countdown and the companion's pose carry everything the chime does.
  • prefers-reduced-motion swaps every bounce and pop for a plain fade. The ladder still reads through colour and mouth shape.
  • Both buttons are real focusable controls with a visible focus ring against the grey ground. Tab order: primary, then secondary.
  • Text meets WCAG AA on both white and Secondary Grey — which in practice means grey type on white and white type on grey. See the contrast note above: four of the five palette colours are fills, not text colours.
  • Urgency is never colour-only. Mouth, posture, and the countdown number each carry it independently.

Stack

Electron 31, no bundler, no framework, no build step. npm start runs the app. Three source files in the main process, one HTML file in the renderer.

The constraint is deliberate: this is a single-user desktop utility that must be able to seize the screen and survive sleep/wake. Electron gives us OS-level window control and the keychain; a web app gives us neither. Adding a build step would buy nothing at this size.

Process model

Main process
src/main.js
  • Owns the poll loop and every alert timer
  • Decides when an alert fires — the renderer never does
  • Holds dismissedEventIds and lastFiredEventId in memory
  • Fires an OS Notification alongside the in-app alert
Bridge
src/preload.js
  • contextIsolation: true, nodeIntegration: false
  • Exposes a narrow, named API — no raw ipcRenderer
Renderer
src/renderer/index.html
  • Pure presentation: setup, home, alert overlay
  • Owns the live countdown tick and the chime
  • Holds no calendar state of its own
Persistence
src/store.js
  • JSON at userData/involveme-data.json
  • safeStorage encrypt/decrypt, OS keychain backed
  • Falls back to plaintext when the keychain is unavailable
Calendar
src/google.js
  • Loopback OAuth on 127.0.0.1:<random>
  • Token refresh, upcoming-events fetch
  • Scope: calendar.readonly — nothing else

Known sharp edge

dismiss-alert takes no payload and closes over lastFiredEventId in the main process. If two alerts fire close together, the wrong event gets marked dismissed. Pass the eventId explicitly, the way snooze-alert already does.

Sync & alert scheduling

ConstantValueBehaviour
POLL_INTERVAL_MS60 000Full re-fetch from Google, then all timers are torn down and rebuilt from scratch. Idempotent by design — a calendar edit is picked up within a minute.
SNOOZE_MS300 000Defer re-arms a single timer for that event and clears it from the dismissed set.
STALE_EVENT_GRACE_MS60 000An event whose alert time has already passed still fires once — but only if the event itself started less than a minute ago. Prevents a wake-from-sleep alert storm.

Access tokens refresh lazily, 60s before expiry, on the next poll that needs one.

Auth & storage

  • Loopback OAuth on a random 127.0.0.1 port — Google's "Desktop app" client type allows this without pre-registering the port.
  • Read-only scope. calendar.readonly means InvolveMe can see events and cannot touch them. Say this on the connect screen, not just in the README.
  • Encrypted at rest via safeStorage, backed by the OS keychain. Nothing leaves the machine except direct calls to Google.
  • The user supplies their own client ID and secret. Acceptable for a personal build; a real distribution needs a shipped client and a verified consent screen.

Risks

  • Token expiry in testing mode. An unpublished consent screen makes Google revoke the refresh token after ~7 days. Today that surfaces as a silent console.error and events quietly stop arriving. The app must detect it and show a plain "reconnect" state — a companion that goes quiet forever is worse than no companion.
  • Sleep and wake. setTimeout does not survive suspend reliably. The one-minute poll rebuilds every timer, so the worst case is a one-minute-late alert — acceptable, but it means the grace window must never be widened.
  • Takeover as an attack surface on attention. A window that grabs focus fullscreen will eventually do it over a screen share or a call. Suppress the takeover while another app is in fullscreen presentation, and fall back to the OS notification.
  • Single points of state. dismissedEventIds lives in memory and resets on relaunch. A restart within an event's window re-fires its alert.
